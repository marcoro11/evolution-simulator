    How Primordia works

    Every creature carries a genome: ten genes (size, speed, senses, fur, heat tolerance, drought tolerance, armor, diet, litter size, longevity) plus two color genes. Genes decide what it looks like and how much energy it burns.

    1. Eat. Creatures wander, find plants (or prey) and gain energy. Every tick costs energy — more for big bodies, fast legs, sharp senses, armor…
    2. Survive. Cold, heat, dry air, thin oxygen and predators all drain energy or kill. Fur and big bodies keep heat in; big ears and sweat glands let it out; camouflage hides you from predators.
    3. Reproduce. Well-fed adults have offspring that inherit their genes with small random mutations. Radiation raises the mutation rate.
    4. Repeat. Whoever fits the environment best leaves the most offspring — so the average creature slowly changes. That’s natural selection.

    Things to try

    • Turn on ⚖️ Compare and give the same rabbits a meadow and a tundra. Watch fur and coat color split.
    • Add wolves to rabbits and watch an arms race (or a collapse).
    • Crank oxygen to 33% for beetles: hello, giant insects.
    • Drop a ☄️ meteor or start an ❄️ ice age mid-run and see who recovers.
    • Click any creature in the world to inspect it.
    • Pick an 🏝️ archipelago or a 🌊 river, switch to 💞 mating and raise the north–south difference: separated populations drift apart into new species.
    • Hit ＋ Design your own to build a creature from scratch.

    Cloning vs mating

    With cloning every baby is a copy of one parent (plus mutations). With mating a parent needs a nearby partner of the same species, and the baby gets a mix of both genomes. Mixing keeps a population together — so new species mostly appear when groups get separated (by water, mountains or distance) and drift too far apart to interbreed. If no partner shows up for a long time, a lonely creature can fall back on self-fertilising.

    Reading the screen

    • The top of each world is colder, the bottom warmer — creatures migrate to where they’re comfortable.
    • Dark shapes with red eyes are predators (the environment’s, they don’t evolve).
    • The timelapse shows the most common species at each moment; drag the slider to scrub through time.
